The Mattress Warranty Reality Check

Updated: August 2026
By Harry Soul — SleepWiseReviews
Independent Sleep Researcher & Editor · Updated August 2026

"10-year warranty." "Sag guarantee." "Non-prorated replacement." These phrases sound like protection, and salespeople use them that way. What they don't tell you on the showroom floor is the specific measured threshold your mattress has to fail before any of that protection actually applies — and real complaint records show that threshold is exactly where most denied claims get stuck.

The short version: A visible sag in your mattress does not automatically qualify for a warranty replacement. It has to clear a specific measured depth — and that depth is different, and mostly undisclosed at the point of sale, brand to brand.

The Sag Threshold Nobody Tells You Before You Buy

Warranty claims for sagging are evaluated against a hard number, measured in inches, not against whether the mattress looks or feels sunken to you.

📏
1.5 inches
The sag threshold used by Sealy, Stearns & Foster, Beautyrest, Serta, and Mattress Firm's house brands. A mattress has to sag this deep — not merely feel soft or dipped — before a claim is approved.
📏
0.75 inches — Tempur-Pedic
Tempur-Pedic's own published warranty covers "a visible indentation greater than three-fourths (0.75) of an inch" that isn't caused by an improper or unsupportive foundation — roughly half the depth required at the brands above.

Sources: mattressnut.com (Mattress Firm warranty breakdown by brand) and Tempur-Pedic's published Limited Warranty terms.

How Real Claims Actually Get Denied

The BBB complaint record for Kingsdown, Inc. shows what this looks like in practice — specific, documented denials, not hypothetical fine print.

Measured just under the line

Multiple complaints describe denials because the measured sag came in under Kingsdown's stated 1.5-inch threshold. In one case, a customer's mattress measured exactly 1 inch of sag, and the company's response was that the mattress was "recovering within the guidelines of the warranty" — not defective, by the terms of the warranty itself.

Foundation and frame requirements

Kingsdown told at least one complainant that its warranty requires "an appropriate frame consisting of a center support and a center leg resting firmly on the floor." A mattress on the wrong foundation type can be denied coverage regardless of how much it has actually sagged — a structural detail most buyers never check against their own bed frame before filing a claim.

Staining as an automatic disqualifier

Several Kingsdown complaints were denied over stains rather than sag. The company's stated reasoning: a stained mattress "will absorb the penetration and begin to compress" — treating any stain as grounds to void the sag warranty, regardless of whether the stain caused or relates to the sagging at all.

Source: BBB — Kingsdown, Inc. USA complaint profile

The practical impact of the brand-by-brand threshold split: a mattress from one of the five brands that require 1.5 inches of sag "has to fail twice as badly" as a Tempur-Pedic before a claim clears the bar for replacement.

What This Means Before You Buy

✓ Ask what foundation or frame is required under warranty before you buy. An incompatible base can void sag coverage entirely, regardless of how much the mattress actually sags.
✓ Find the exact sag threshold, in inches, for your specific model before you sign anything. It's in the warranty document, not the sales pitch, and it can run anywhere from 0.75" to 1.5" or more depending on the brand.
✓ Measure and photograph any sag with a straightedge and ruler as soon as you notice it, and keep dated photos. Several denied BBB complaints turned on measurements that landed just under the line.
✓ Protect the mattress from stains from day one. A stain can void a structural sag warranty even when it has nothing to do with the sagging itself.
✓ Don't read "non-prorated" or "lifetime" as "easy to claim." That language describes what you get paid if a claim is approved — not how hard it is to get approved in the first place.

"A warranty is only as good as the threshold behind it. If you don't know that number before you buy, you're not actually covered — you're hoping."
