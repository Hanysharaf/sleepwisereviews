    Does Magnesium Help You Sleep? What the Research Says

    Magnesium is an essential mineral involved in hundreds of biochemical reactions in the body. Whether taking extra improves sleep is much less certain: the evidence is limited.

    What the Science Says

    A small 2012 randomized trial in older adults with insomnia reported improvements in some sleep measures. Overall, though, the evidence that magnesium supplements improve sleep is limited and low quality: there are only a few small studies, many in older adults, so the results may not apply to everyone. Evidence for any one form being better for sleep is thin.

    Researchers have suggested that magnesium may influence calming brain signaling and stress hormones, but these ideas come largely from lab and animal work and have not been shown to translate into better sleep for most people.

    Worth knowing: Dietary surveys suggest many US adults take in less magnesium from food than recommended, according to the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements. Nuts, seeds, leafy greens and whole grains are common food sources. If you think you may be low, ask a doctor rather than assuming.

    Check the Label for Magnesium Oxide

    The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements notes that magnesium oxide is absorbed less completely than forms such as citrate, and it commonly acts as a laxative. Check the supplement facts to see which form you are buying.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Does magnesium help you sleep?
    Possibly, but the evidence is limited. A small trial in older adults with insomnia reported benefit, but reviews have rated the overall evidence as low quality, and it is unclear whether people who are not low in magnesium benefit. Glycinate is the form usually marketed for sleep, but it has not been shown to work better than other forms. See our natural sleep aids guide for how magnesium compares with other options.
    How long does magnesium take to work for sleep?
    There is no reliable timeline, and many people notice no change. Research is too limited to say how long to try it. If you do, follow the label and see a doctor if sleep problems continue for more than a few weeks.
    Can I take magnesium with melatonin?
    There is not enough research to say whether combining them helps. Both are sold together in some sleep products, but if you take medication or have a health condition, ask a doctor or pharmacist before combining supplements.
    What's the difference between magnesium glycinate and bisglycinate?
    Labels use the terms loosely. Both refer to magnesium bound to the amino acid glycine; bisglycinate means two glycine molecules per magnesium. Neither has been shown to be better for sleep than other forms, so focus on the elemental magnesium per serving.
    Is magnesium safe to take every night?
    Many adults take magnesium supplements daily, but the NIH sets an upper limit of 350 mg per day for magnesium from supplements and medicines in adults (this does not apply to food). Higher amounts commonly cause diarrhea, nausea and cramps. People with kidney problems, and those taking certain medications, should ask a doctor first. Research on long-term nightly use for sleep is limited.
    Can magnesium cause vivid dreams?
    Some people report vivid dreams with various supplements, but we are not aware of solid research showing that magnesium causes them. If you notice troubling side effects, stop and ask a doctor.
    Which magnesium form is best for sleep overall?
    There is no proven best form. Evidence that magnesium helps sleep at all is limited, and studies have not shown glycinate or threonate to work better than other forms. Our editorial picks start with glycinate because it is the form most often marketed for sleep, not because it is proven better. The NIH notes that magnesium oxide is absorbed less completely than other forms and commonly acts as a laxative.
