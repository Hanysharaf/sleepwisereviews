What the Research Says About Magnesium Glycinate and Sleep

Magnesium: Magnesium is an essential mineral involved in hundreds of enzyme reactions. A small 2012 randomized trial in older adults with insomnia reported improvements in some sleep measures, but overall the evidence that magnesium supplements improve sleep is limited and low quality. Most studies are small and many involved older adults, so results may not apply to everyone, and it is unclear whether people who are not low in magnesium benefit.

Glycine: Glycine, the amino acid in magnesium glycinate, has been studied on its own for sleep in small trials, but those used doses that may differ from the amount of glycine in a magnesium supplement, and the evidence is limited.

Glycinate versus other forms: Whether glycinate works better than other magnesium forms for sleep has not been established. Glycinate is the form most often marketed for sleep.

Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

Elemental Magnesium Content — The Number That Matters

Labels can list the total compound weight (for example, 400mg magnesium glycinate) separately from the elemental magnesium, which is the amount that counts toward limits. Read the "elemental magnesium" line in the Supplement Facts panel. The NIH upper limit for magnesium from supplements and medicines in adults is 350 mg per day (this does not include magnesium from food), and higher amounts commonly cause diarrhea, nausea and cramps.

Third-Party Testing

Certification programs such as NSF and USP check whether a product contains what its label says; they do not show that it improves sleep. A certification can be a useful tie-breaker, especially for daily use. Confirm on the certifier's own website that a product is listed.

Form: Powder vs. Capsule vs. Tablet

Powder allows flexible dosing but needs mixing. Capsules are convenient for travel. Tablets are often cheaper but can be large. Choose whichever you will take consistently; there is no proof that one format improves sleep more than another.

When to Take It

There is no established best time to take magnesium for sleep. Taking it with food and splitting the amount across the day are commonly suggested ways to reduce stomach upset. Follow the label, and lower the dose if you get diarrhea.

Magnesium Glycinate vs. Other Sleep Supplements

Magnesium glycinate is not a sedative, and the evidence for its effect on sleep is limited. For ongoing trouble sleeping, cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is the best-supported option; see our natural sleep aids guide for how magnesium compares with melatonin and other supplements. Talk to a doctor before combining supplements, if you take medication, or if you have kidney problems.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is magnesium glycinate better for sleep than other forms?

Not proven. Glycinate is the form most often marketed for sleep, but studies have not shown it works better than other magnesium forms, and evidence that magnesium improves sleep at all is limited. Glycine, the amino acid it is bound to, has been studied separately in small trials. Choose a form based on the label dose, price and how your stomach tolerates it.

What is the right dosage for sleep?

There is no established dose for sleep. The NIH upper limit for magnesium from supplements is 350 mg per day for adults (this does not apply to food), and amounts above that commonly cause diarrhea, nausea and cramps. Check the elemental magnesium on the Supplement Facts panel, follow the label, and ask a doctor if you have kidney problems or take medication.

How long does it take to work?

There is no reliable timeline, and many people notice no change. Research is too limited to say how long to try it. If you do, follow the label and see a doctor if sleep problems continue for more than a few weeks.

Can you take magnesium glycinate every night?

Many adults take magnesium supplements daily within the NIH upper limit of 350 mg per day from supplements. Higher amounts commonly cause diarrhea, nausea and cramps. Magnesium is cleared by the kidneys, so people with kidney problems should not use supplements without a doctor's advice. Research on long-term nightly use for sleep is limited.

What is the difference between magnesium glycinate and bisglycinate?

Labels use the terms loosely. Both refer to magnesium bound to the amino acid glycine; bisglycinate means two glycine molecules per magnesium. Focus on the elemental magnesium per serving rather than the naming.

Our Bottom Line

Evidence that any magnesium supplement improves sleep is limited, so treat this as a low-risk trial rather than a fix. If you want to try a glycinate form, Thorne Magnesium Bisglycinate is our editorial first pick for its short ingredient list and listed certification. Pure Encapsulations is an option if you avoid common allergens, and Doctor's Best is a low-cost option.

Keep total supplemental magnesium under the NIH limit of 350 mg per day, ask a doctor first if you have kidney problems or take medication, and see a doctor if sleep trouble lasts more than a few weeks.
