    How to Choose the Right Weighted Blanket

    Weight

    Sleep Foundation says sleepers tend to feel most comfortable under a blanket of roughly 10 percent of their body weight, and that for a healthy adult a comfortable blanket can weigh between 5 and 12 percent. It recommends trying different weights to find the right level of compression.

    Material

    If weighted blankets have felt too hot before, an open-knit design is worth trying: Sleep Foundation reports that many sleepers find them more breathable, and recommends one for exceptionally hot or humid climates.

    Fill Type

    Check the product listing for the fill material and the care instructions before you buy; fill and construction differ between brands.

    🛏️ Pro tip: If you sleep with a partner who doesn't want a weighted blanket, get a Twin or Full size just for your side of the bed.

    FAQ

    Do weighted blankets actually help with sleep?
    The evidence is limited. A 2020 randomized trial in the Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine (Ekholm et al.) gave 120 patients with insomnia and depression, bipolar disorder, generalized anxiety disorder or ADHD either a weighted metal chain blanket or a light plastic chain blanket for 4 weeks; insomnia severity ratings were significantly better with the weighted blanket, and the authors reported no serious adverse events. That trial tested chain blankets in a clinical group, so it does not show the same result for the blankets reviewed here or for people without these diagnoses. Sleep Foundation describes the wider research as still fairly inconclusive.
    Are weighted blankets safe?
    Not for everyone. Sleep Foundation advises avoiding weighted blankets, or consulting a physician first, if you have a breathing disorder such as asthma or COPD, obstructive sleep apnea, certain cardiovascular conditions, or claustrophobia. It says they are generally safe for children who are at least 3 years old and weigh at least 50 pounds, and that any weighted blanket can pose a suffocation risk to younger or smaller children.
    Can I wash it at home?
    Check the care label. Sleep Foundation says you should first make sure your washer and dryer can handle the extra weight, that commercial-grade appliances or a laundromat may be necessary, and that some inserts are spot-clean only.
    Sources & further reading

    The evidence for weighted blankets is promising but still limited. Sleep Foundation calls the research behind the purported benefits fairly inconclusive, and the trial cited below tested chain blankets in patients with psychiatric diagnoses. The "10% of body weight" figure is Sleep Foundation's guiding rule, not a clinical-trial result.
